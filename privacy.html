Privacy

What we fetch, what we store, what we don't.

Last updated 23 October 2026.

What the scanner fetches

When you scan a domain, or a Pro key watches it, Citehound fetches public files from that domain: robots.txt, sitemap.xml, llms.txt, /.well-known/ucp, and the homepage. If the site looks like a store, a scan also reads one product sitemap and one product page, made with a user agent that names Citehound and only where robots.txt allows it. All of it is public by definition — nothing behind a login, nothing private. We fetch whatever domain you type in, whether or not it's yours.

The two free tools fetch less. The llms.txt checker reads one file, llms.txt, from the domain you enter. The schema generator reads that domain's homepage for its title, description and language. Neither keeps what it reads, and both share the scanner's rate limit described below.

What we store

The free scanner and the free tools store almost nothing. There are no accounts and no scan history, and the scanner and the tools collect no email address. A scan result exists in your browser for as long as the page stays open. The Pro waitlist and Pro reports are the exceptions, described in their own sections below.

A second exception is the older full-site crawl endpoint, which is not linked from the site but whose address is open. A crawl stores the domain, the list of pages it chose, and each page’s result, under a random 128-bit id, for 90 days after its last step. Nothing about who started it is stored with it, and nobody can list or search crawls: you need the id.

To stop abuse, we keep a keyed one-way hash of your IP address paired with a request counter, purely to enforce a rate limit. It expires automatically within a day (the hourly counter within an hour), isn't linked to which domains were scanned, and is never stored as a raw IP address.

The MCP server keeps short-lived counters keyed by the scanned domain, kept for an hour, to limit repeated fetches of one site. They hold no caller identity. The Citehound GitHub Action’s endpoint does the same with one-way hashes of the scanned domain and of the caller’s IP address, also kept for an hour; it receives the domain and nothing else, and no repository name.

When the scanner cannot fetch a page, it writes the address it tried and the reason to our server log, to help us fix problems. That log line holds no IP address and no scan result.

Separately, we count visits from known AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and similar) to our own site, so we can publish real data on how often they show up. This is two aggregate counts: visits per crawler per day, and how often each page of our site was requested by those crawlers. It is never a per-visit log, and nothing about human visitors is recorded at all.

Pro reports

If you order a Citehound Pro report, we collect three things from you: your name, your email address and the address of the site you want scanned. We use them only to deliver your report and for support. If you press “Email me this report”, we send the report link to the address you gave us, and to no other.

The crawl stores the site’s domain, the list of pages it chose and each page’s result, under a random 128-bit id, for 90 days. That record contains no name and no email address. Your name and email are kept in a separate order record, for 90 days from the day you start the report. A lookup from the report to its order exists only so the email button can find the address you gave us. If a report includes citation questions, its record also holds a short profile of what the site’s pages say it is and the questions written from it, for the same 90 days. If it includes a citation check, the record also holds, for each question asked, whether the site’s domain was cited, whether the brand name was in the answer, the other cited domains, the model and the date, and the cost of the call as a number. It holds no answer text, and no name or email address. Both records delete themselves when the 90 days end. A link you never use expires after 30 days. If a scan cannot read any page of the site, your link is given back so you can try again, and your details stay in the order record until it is used or expires. Some Pro reports are made for people we invite to try Pro; they work the same way, and the order record also notes that it was an invitation and a short label we chose for it, never your contact details.

At the end of a report you can leave optional feedback: a number from 1 to 5 and up to 1,000 characters of text. It is stored for 90 days under the report’s random id, with no name and no email address, and only we read it. Please leave personal details out of the text.

We do not sell your details and we do not share them. They are stored with our database host, Upstash. When you press “Email me this report”, your email address and the report link go to our email provider, Resend, to be delivered, and nothing else does. A Pro report’s summary is written by rules here: no page content, page address, domain or personal detail is sent to a model. The citation questions and the citation check are the one exception, described under Third parties below.

Anyone who has a report’s link can open it, so treat the link like a password. We do not publish report links or list them anywhere, and report pages ask search engines not to index them and send no referrer. To have a report and its order record deleted before the 90 days end, write to hey@getcitehound.com with the report link.

The Pro waitlist

If you join the Citehound Pro waitlist on the Pro page, we store your email address, the name if you gave one, the date, and the version of the consent wording you ticked (“Tell me when Citehound Pro opens”). The record sits under a keyed one-way hash of your address, so the same address is never listed twice, and it is deleted automatically after 12 months. We use it for one thing: to write to you when Pro opens. We do not sell it or share it.

When you join, we send one confirmation message through our email provider, Resend, which receives your address and the message to deliver it. We send at most one such message to an address in 30 days. The message contains no tracking pixel and no tracked links, and every message has a “Remove me from the list” link. Opening that link shows a page with one button; pressing it deletes your record at once. You can also write to hey@getcitehound.com and we will remove you. The form never tells anyone whether an address is already on the list.

The homepage also has a short form for early access to Fix and Beyond, our planned services. It uses the same list. It asks for your email address and a box agreeing that we may email you about the request, and it takes the website and a one-line note if you add them. We store those with your address and which of Pro, Fix or Beyond you asked about, for the same 12 months, and use them only to answer you. That form sends no automatic confirmation message; we reply by hand. To have the record deleted, write to hey@getcitehound.com.

The form uses the same abuse limit as everything else here: a keyed one-way hash of your IP address and a counter, kept for an hour. The address is not stored.

API keys

If you ask for a Citehound API key on the API page, we use your email address once, to send you the key. We store a one-way hash of the key, a one-way hash of your email address, a masked form of the address (for example “j***@example.com”, which the API’s account endpoint shows), the plan and the date the key was created. We do not store the key, or your address, in readable form. A key record is kept until the key is revoked. When you revoke a key, the address hash and the masked address are removed at once, and the rest of the record, which holds only the key’s hash, its plan and its dates, expires 90 days later. You can revoke a key with the link in the key email. To have your key records deleted sooner, write to hey@getcitehound.com.

If you tick the box for product updates on the key form, we also store your address, the date and the source (the key form), apart from the key record, for 12 months, and use it only to send you product updates. The box is off unless you tick it. To be removed sooner, write to hey@getcitehound.com. The key email is transactional: it carries the key, and no marketing and no tracking.

To limit abuse, the API counts requests with one-way hashes of the key, of the domain you ask about and of your network address. The hourly counters expire within two hours and the daily ones within two days. They hold no readable key, address or domain. We do not log your key, your address or the requests you make. The key email goes through our email provider, Resend, which receives your address and the message to deliver it.

If a Pro key watches a domain, a scheduled job fetches that domain’s public files, the same ones as a scan, about every 7 days. We store the domain with the key’s id, a snapshot of each scan (the score, the pillar scores, the ids of the checks that failed, the state of ten AI crawlers, and whether llms.txt and structured data were present) and the changes between scans. We keep the snapshots and the changes for up to 365 days, and delete them earlier when the last key watching the domain stops watching it or is revoked. If you give an address for alerts, we store it in readable form, with the date, only to send those alerts: at most one email a day for each domain, each with a one-click link that stops them for that domain. We delete the address when you stop watching your last domain or revoke the key.

If you add a webhook, we store its https address, the event types you chose and the secret that signs the deliveries. The secret is stored encrypted and is shown once. We also keep a log of the last 50 deliveries (time, event, status and HTTP status, not the content) for 90 days. Webhooks are deleted when you delete them or revoke the key. A receiver sees the address of our host, not yours.

The State of AI Readiness report

On the page for our State of AI Readiness report you can ask for the PDF by email. The form asks for your email address and one box, “Send me the report”, which you must tick. A second box, “Also send occasional Citehound updates”, is separate, optional and unticked unless you tick it. We store your address, the time you asked, the version of the wording you saw and whether you ticked the second box. It is a separate list from the Pro waitlist. The record sits under a keyed one-way hash of your address, so the same address is never listed twice, and it is deleted automatically after 12 months. We use it to email you the link to the report, so that you can ask again, and, only if you ticked the second box, for occasional Citehound updates. We do not sell it or share it.

The email contains a link to the PDF. The link works for 30 days, then you can ask for a new one. It carries a signed token with a record id and an expiry date, and no address. The PDF is not on this site as a file: it sits in our database and is handed out only to a valid link, with search engines asked not to index it and no referrer sent. We send at most three report emails a day to one address and a fixed number a day in all. The email is delivered by our email provider, Resend, which receives your address and the message. It contains no tracking pixel and no tracked links. Every email has a “Remove my address” link and the standard unsubscribe header. Opening the link shows a page with one button; pressing it deletes your record at once and stops any link sent to it from working. You can also write to hey@getcitehound.com and we will remove you. The form never tells anyone whether an address is already on the list.

The form uses the same abuse limit as everything else here: a keyed one-way hash of your IP address and a counter, kept for an hour. The address is not stored. We keep daily totals of how many people asked for the report, how many emails went out, how many times the PDF was served and how many people removed themselves, as plain numbers with no person attached.

Fix and Beyond projects

When you ask for Fix or Beyond, the early-access form stores what it asks for: your email address, the site and a short note, as described under the Pro waitlist. We reply by hand. We keep that correspondence, and the quotes we send, for 24 months.

When a project starts, we make a private workspace for it, at an address made of a long random id. For Fix the workspace holds the scan results, the list of fixes and their status. For Beyond it holds the market questions, the gaps, the pieces of content in their stages, your comments on them and the monthly measurements. It holds no name and no email address. The workspace has no field for passwords or access keys, so do not put them in a comment or in the request form.

Anyone who has a workspace link can open it, so treat it like a password. We do not publish the links, and workspace pages ask search engines not to index them and send no referrer. When you press Verify, approve a piece or leave a comment, it is saved in the workspace. To limit repeated use we keep a keyed one-way hash of your IP address and a counter for an hour; the address is not stored.

A workspace is kept for 400 days after its last activity. When we archive a finished project, it is deleted 90 days later. To have one deleted sooner, write to hey@getcitehound.com with its link. Workspaces are stored with Upstash. Our own admin page for managing them is protected by a token and sets a sign-in cookie, for us only.

Payments

Payments are not open yet. When they open, you will pay on our payment provider’s hosted checkout page, so your card details go to the provider and not to us. The provider tells us your name, your email address and an order reference, and we keep them in the order record described under Pro reports. We keep invoice and billing records for 5 years. Our payment provider may keep its own records for longer.

Deleting your data

To have your data deleted, write to hey@getcitehound.com from the address you gave us, and include the report or workspace link if you have one. We delete the matching records and tell you when it is done. The waitlist and the report list also have their own removal links.

What we don't collect

The free scanner and tools collect no personal data: no email addresses, no accounts, no advertising trackers. The only personal details we hold are the ones you give us when you join the Pro waitlist, ask for the State of AI Readiness report, ask for an API key or order a Pro report, described above. This site’s pages set no cookies and load no advertising script and no tracker that follows you across other sites. They count page views with Vercel Web Analytics, described under Third parties, which sets no cookies. The one exception is the sign-in cookie of our own admin page, which is set only when we sign in. Two small notices on the homepage, the Pro waitlist bar and the report card, remember in your browser, for that visit only, that you closed them. That uses your browser’s session storage on your device; nothing about it is sent to us.

Third parties

Hosting: Vercel. The site and the scan function both run on Vercel's infrastructure, and Vercel handles the requests that reach them as any host does, under its own terms. Vercel also keeps its own request logs, which include the IP addresses of visitors, under its own terms and for the periods it sets. Vercel also keeps its own request logs, which include the IP addresses of visitors, for the periods it sets; we do not follow visitors with them.

Storage: Upstash holds the records described above (rate-limit counters, crawl results, the Pro waitlist, the report list, the State of AI Readiness PDF itself, API key records, the Pro order and report records, feedback, and the daily totals).

Email: Resend delivers four kinds of message: the report link a Pro customer asks for, the one confirmation message a waitlist signup gets, the link to the State of AI Readiness PDF and the API key a person asks for, each to the address the person gave us. It receives nothing else.

Executive summaries: when a crawl run through the older endpoint finishes, its executive summary may be written from aggregated figures: scores, counts of pages and the names of the checks. Those figures are sent to a model provider, Google’s Gemini API, to be phrased. No page content, no page addresses, no domain name and no personal data are sent. Google’s terms for unpaid use of the API may allow it to use what it receives to improve its products. If the model is unavailable, or its reply fails our checks, the summary is written by rules here and nothing is sent.

Citation questions and citation check (Pro, only when a report includes them): to understand a site and write questions about it, excerpts of that site’s public page text (up to 12,000 characters, from the homepage and up to four key pages) go to Anthropic, which runs the Claude model; no web search is used for this step, and nothing about you goes with the text: no name, email address, token, order or report id. When a report includes a citation check, each of about ten of the generated questions (the question text and a language tag, nothing else: no site text, no domain, no personal detail) is sent to Anthropic again with Claude’s web search tool, and the cited pages come back. Anthropic’s commercial terms say it does not train its models on this content. Citehound keeps, for the 90 days of the report, only whether the site’s domain was cited, whether the brand name appears in the answer, the other cited domains with a link, the model name and the date: not the answer text. The results are a dated sample from one provider, one run per question, not a ranking. For the citation check, Anthropic is the only model provider used today. If we start using another one, for example OpenAI for a ChatGPT sample, we will update this page first.

Payment provider: not in use yet. When payments open, our payment provider will take the payment on its own hosted checkout page and will tell us the buyer’s name, email address and an order reference.

Analytics: the pages count page views with Vercel Web Analytics. It sets no cookies. Vercel says that it collects no identifiers that follow visitors across other websites or applications, and that visitors are told apart for a day by a hash made from the request, which is discarded after 24 hours. Each page view records the page address, the referrer, the visitor’s country and region, the type of device, the system and the browser, and it is used for aggregated counts. A shared scan link carries the scanned domain in its address, so that domain is part of the recorded page address. Beyond that, the only visit counts we keep are the aggregate crawler counts described above. The Pro system also keeps daily totals of its own events: how many people joined the waitlist or asked for the State of AI Readiness report, or removed themselves from either, how many order links were issued, how many reports were started, finished, partly finished or failed, and how many messages were sent. They are plain numbers with no person, address, link or report attached, and nothing in your browser feeds them.
