Privacy

What we fetch, what we store, what we don't.

Last updated 11 October 2026.

What the scanner fetches

When you scan a domain, Citehound fetches public files from that domain: robots.txt, sitemap.xml, llms.txt, /.well-known/ucp, and the homepage. If the site looks like a store, a scan also reads one product sitemap and one product page, made with a user agent that names Citehound and only where robots.txt allows it. All of it is public by definition — nothing behind a login, nothing private. We fetch whatever domain you type in, whether or not it's yours.

The two free tools fetch less. The llms.txt checker reads one file, llms.txt, from the domain you enter. The schema generator reads that domain's homepage for its title, description and language. Neither keeps what it reads, and both share the scanner's rate limit described below.

What we store

The free scanner and the free tools store almost nothing. There are no accounts and no scan history, and the scanner and the tools collect no email address. A scan result exists in your browser for as long as the page stays open. The Pro waitlist and Pro reports are the exceptions, described in their own sections below.

A second exception is the older full-site crawl endpoint, which is not linked from the site but whose address is open. A crawl stores the domain, the list of pages it chose, and each page’s result, under a random 128-bit id, for 90 days after its last step. Nothing about who started it is stored with it, and nobody can list or search crawls: you need the id.

To stop abuse, we keep a keyed one-way hash of your IP address paired with a request counter, purely to enforce a rate limit. It expires automatically within a day (the hourly counter within an hour), isn't linked to which domains were scanned, and is never stored as a raw IP address.

The MCP server keeps short-lived counters keyed by the scanned domain, kept for an hour, to limit repeated fetches of one site. They hold no caller identity.

Separately, we count visits from known AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and similar) to our own site, so we can publish real data on how often they show up. This is two aggregate counts: visits per crawler per day, and how often each page of our site was requested by those crawlers. It is never a per-visit log, and nothing about human visitors is recorded at all.

Pro reports

If you order a Citehound Pro report, we collect three things from you: your name, your email address and the address of the site you want scanned. We use them only to deliver your report and for support. If you press “Email me this report”, we send the report link to the address you gave us, and to no other.

The crawl stores the site’s domain, the list of pages it chose and each page’s result, under a random 128-bit id, for 90 days. That record contains no name and no email address. Your name and email are kept in a separate order record, for 90 days from the day you start the report. A lookup from the report to its order exists only so the email button can find the address you gave us. If a report includes citation questions, its record also holds a short profile of what the site’s pages say it is and the questions written from it, for the same 90 days. If it includes a citation check, the record also holds, for each question asked, whether the site’s domain was cited, whether the brand name was in the answer, the other cited domains, the model and the date, and the cost of the call as a number. It holds no answer text, and no name or email address. Both records delete themselves when the 90 days end. A link you never use expires after 30 days. If a scan cannot read any page of the site, your link is given back so you can try again, and your details stay in the order record until it is used or expires. Some Pro reports are made for people we invite to try Pro; they work the same way, and the order record also notes that it was an invitation and a short label we chose for it, never your contact details.

At the end of a report you can leave optional feedback: a number from 1 to 5 and up to 1,000 characters of text. It is stored for 90 days under the report’s random id, with no name and no email address, and only we read it. Please leave personal details out of the text.

We do not sell your details and we do not share them. They are stored with our database host, Upstash. When you press “Email me this report”, your email address and the report link go to our email provider, Resend, to be delivered, and nothing else does. A Pro report’s summary is written by rules here: no page content, page address, domain or personal detail is sent to a model. The citation questions and the citation check are the one exception, described under Third parties below.

Anyone who has a report’s link can open it, so treat the link like a password. We do not publish report links or list them anywhere, and report pages ask search engines not to index them and send no referrer. To have a report and its order record deleted before the 90 days end, write to hey@getcitehound.com with the report link.

The Pro waitlist

If you join the Citehound Pro waitlist on the Pro page, we store your email address, the name if you gave one, the date, and the version of the consent wording you ticked (“Tell me when Citehound Pro opens”). The record sits under a keyed one-way hash of your address, so the same address is never listed twice, and it is deleted automatically after 12 months. We use it for one thing: to write to you when Pro opens. We do not sell it or share it.

When you join, we send one confirmation message through our email provider, Resend, which receives your address and the message to deliver it. We send at most one such message to an address in 30 days. The message contains no tracking pixel and no tracked links, and every message has a “Remove me from the list” link. Opening that link shows a page with one button; pressing it deletes your record at once. You can also write to hey@getcitehound.com and we will remove you. The form never tells anyone whether an address is already on the list.

The homepage also has a short form for early access to Fix and Beyond, our planned services. It uses the same list. It asks for your email address and a box agreeing that we may email you about the request, and it takes the website and a one-line note if you add them. We store those with your address and which of Pro, Fix or Beyond you asked about, for the same 12 months, and use them only to answer you. That form sends no automatic confirmation message; we reply by hand. To have the record deleted, write to hey@getcitehound.com.

The form uses the same abuse limit as everything else here: a keyed one-way hash of your IP address and a counter, kept for an hour. The address is not stored.

The State of AI Readiness report

On the page for our State of AI Readiness report you can ask for the PDF by email. The form asks for your email address and one box, “Send me the report”, which you must tick. A second box, “Also send occasional Citehound updates”, is separate, optional and unticked unless you tick it. We store your address, the time you asked, the version of the wording you saw and whether you ticked the second box. It is a separate list from the Pro waitlist. The record sits under a keyed one-way hash of your address, so the same address is never listed twice, and it is deleted automatically after 12 months. We use it to email you the link to the report, so that you can ask again, and, only if you ticked the second box, for occasional Citehound updates. We do not sell it or share it.

The email contains a link to the PDF. The link works for 30 days, then you can ask for a new one. It carries a signed token with a record id and an expiry date, and no address. The PDF is not on this site as a file: it sits in our database and is handed out only to a valid link, with search engines asked not to index it and no referrer sent. We send at most three report emails a day to one address and a fixed number a day in all. The email is delivered by our email provider, Resend, which receives your address and the message. It contains no tracking pixel and no tracked links. Every email has a “Remove my address” link and the standard unsubscribe header. Opening the link shows a page with one button; pressing it deletes your record at once and stops any link sent to it from working. You can also write to hey@getcitehound.com and we will remove you. The form never tells anyone whether an address is already on the list.

The form uses the same abuse limit as everything else here: a keyed one-way hash of your IP address and a counter, kept for an hour. The address is not stored. We keep daily totals of how many people asked for the report, how many emails went out, how many times the PDF was served and how many people removed themselves, as plain numbers with no person attached.

What we don't collect

The free scanner and tools collect no personal data: no email addresses, no accounts, no advertising trackers. The only personal details we hold are the ones you give us when you join the Pro waitlist, ask for the State of AI Readiness report or order a Pro report, described above. This site’s pages set no cookies and load no analytics or advertising script. Two small notices on the homepage, the Pro waitlist bar and the report card, remember in your browser, for that visit only, that you closed them. That uses your browser’s session storage on your device; nothing about it is sent to us.

Third parties

Hosting: Vercel. The site and the scan function both run on Vercel's infrastructure, and Vercel handles the requests that reach them as any host does, under its own terms.

Storage: Upstash holds the records described above (rate-limit counters, crawl results, the Pro waitlist, the report list, the State of AI Readiness PDF itself, the Pro order and report records, feedback, and the daily totals).

Email: Resend delivers three kinds of message: the report link a Pro customer asks for, the one confirmation message a waitlist signup gets, and the link to the State of AI Readiness PDF, each to the address the person gave us. It receives nothing else.

Launch Llama badge: the homepage footer loads a badge image from a third party, Launch Llama, which receives the visitor’s IP address and browser details when it loads. No other page loads it.

Typefaces: pages load their typefaces from Google Fonts, so your browser sends its IP address and user agent to Google when a page loads. Private Pro pages do the same but send no referrer.

Executive summaries: when a crawl run through the older endpoint finishes, its executive summary may be written from aggregated figures: scores, counts of pages and the names of the checks. Those figures are sent to a model provider, Google’s Gemini API, to be phrased. No page content, no page addresses, no domain name and no personal data are sent. Google’s terms for unpaid use of the API may allow it to use what it receives to improve its products. If the model is unavailable, or its reply fails our checks, the summary is written by rules here and nothing is sent.

Citation questions and citation check (Pro, only when a report includes them): to understand a site and write questions about it, excerpts of that site’s public page text (up to 12,000 characters, from the homepage and up to four key pages) go to Anthropic, which runs the Claude model; no web search is used for this step, and nothing about you goes with the text: no name, email address, token, order or report id. When a report includes a citation check, each of about ten of the generated questions (the question text and a language tag, nothing else: no site text, no domain, no personal detail) is sent to Anthropic again with Claude’s web search tool, and the cited pages come back. Anthropic’s commercial terms say it does not train its models on this content. Citehound keeps, for the 90 days of the report, only whether the site’s domain was cited, whether the brand name appears in the answer, the other cited domains with a link, the model name and the date: not the answer text. The results are a dated sample from one provider, one run per question, not a ranking.

Analytics: there is none. The pages carry no analytics script, so no tool collects what you read or click. The only visit counts we keep are the aggregate crawler counts described above. The Pro system also keeps daily totals of its own events: how many people joined the waitlist or asked for the State of AI Readiness report, or removed themselves from either, how many order links were issued, how many reports were started, finished, partly finished or failed, and how many messages were sent. They are plain numbers with no person, address, link or report attached, and nothing in your browser feeds them.
