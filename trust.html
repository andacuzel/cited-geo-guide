What you can check.

Who runs Citehound, what the scan measures, what we store and how to report a problem. Each row links to the evidence.

The evidence, in eight places.

  • Who runs it.

    Citehound is built and run by Andaç Üzel. The About page has the background.

    About

  • What the scan measures, and what it does not.

    Citehound measures AI readiness: whether AI crawlers can access your content, and whether your on-page signals give a model a reason to trust and cite you. A perfect score does not guarantee an answer engine will cite you.

    Read the methodology

  • Our own results.

    A crawl of our own site, 28 pages, nothing edited, and the crawl before we fixed what it found: the whole-site score went from 69 to 83.

    See the sample report

  • What we store.

    No accounts, no email list and no scan history. The privacy page lists the few things we do keep and for how long.

    Read the privacy policy

  • Where the data comes from.

    Benchmarks are our own scans of 219 homepages in 6 categories, on stated dates. Each research report publishes its method and its limits.

    See the benchmarks

  • The MCP server.

    13 read-only tools and 3 prompts, no key and no account. The documentation says what the server keeps and what it limits.

    Read the MCP documentation

  • What changed, and when.

    30 changes a visitor or an MCP user can see, newest first, with the commits behind each.

    Read the changelog

  • Report a security issue.

    Email hey@getcitehound.com. The security.txt file lists the contact, the languages we read and when the file expires.
